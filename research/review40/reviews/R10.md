### What exists today that is close to my lens

| mechanism | what it does | why it is not an experience store |
|---|---|---|
| trained profiles (`kolmo train`, `-P`, `plan/profile.c`) | pins the plan per first-line shape / first 32 bytes, mode and size band; dictionaries | needs an explicit training run on a sample; frames record the profile id and **do not decode without the profile** (`KOLMO_ERR_PROFILE`) |
| `--target` + `kolmo calibrate` (`plan/target.c`) | picks a setting per chunk from trial sizes and a speed table per data class | the table is fitted once (`target_table.inc` or `~/.config/kolmo/calibration`); nothing is learnt from real encodes |
| planner trials (`make_plan`) and block trials (`compress_worker`) | sample, try, keep the smaller | every file starts from zero: the same losing trials are paid for again on every file of the same kind |
| tracker B13 "learned selector (RL or bandit) in place of trials" | "Measured, not planned now": at `balanced` all planning is 20.8% of encode | it looked at `balanced` planning only; the block-level trials of `ratio` and `-x` (below) were not part of that ceiling |

So nothing in rc3 remembers anything between two calls. That is the gap I reviewed.

## 2. What I measured

### 2.1 Method

Research build of my clone with two `fprintf`s behind `-DKOLMO_XPSTAT` (plus the existing `-DKOLMO_TIMING`): one
line per `codec_compress` call of 4 KiB or more (codec, level, input bytes, output bytes, ms) and one line per block
with what was finally written. Build: `make build/kolmo CFLAGS='-O3 -march=native -fno-plt -DKOLMO_TIMING
-DKOLMO_XPSTAT'`. Runs: `results/R10-work/run_tax.sh` (`kolmo-xpstat <mode> -T 1 file out`), parsed by
`results/R10-work/tax_parse.py`: a call is **kept** when it is the one whose bytes are the block's payload,
**discarded** otherwise (slice probes and full codings that lost). The frames written by this build are the rc3
frames (the instrumentation prints only).

Reading: of 94.5 s of encode, **24.3 s (25.7%) is coder work whose output is thrown away**: 17.8 s of LZMA2 and 6.5 s
of block sorting. Three files carry most of it:

- **mr** (16-bit image plan, tflag 6): a 512 KiB slice says BWT/LZMA2 = 0.976 (undecided), so LZMA2 codes the whole
  block in 6.69 s (2,171,812 B), then block sorting codes it in 0.82 s (1,989,718 B, 8.4% smaller) and is kept. 88%
  of the encode is discarded; the same 512 KiB slice is block-sorted twice (121,282 B both times) and coded by
  LZMA2 preset 1 twice (124,286 B both times), and a third slice of the plain bytes is sorted for the retry.
- **nci** (log-token plan): LZMA2 over the token stream takes 5.33 s (1,533,871 B) and loses to block sorting of the
  plain bytes (1.78 s, 1,199,578 B). 71% discarded.
- **samba** (7 content segments): 50% discarded; **mozilla** (33 tar members): 4.28 s of block-sorting attempts.

The outcomes repeat by kind of data, which is what makes them learnable. Per-member outcomes of mozilla (same
log, per-block listing in section 2.6): on every one of the 16 members where block sorting was coded in full it
lost, by 13% to 40% (BWT/LZMA2 between 1.134 and 1.401). After three such observations a store would stop paying
for the other thirteen attempts. On samba's binary segments the ratio sits between 0.958 and 1.014, next to the 3%
keep threshold: there history must not be trusted and both codings must still be made (the design below keeps them).

A second, smaller finding from the same logs: on text at `ratio` the planner's word-transform trial (word coding of
2 MiB and two zstd -3 passes) runs after the table/log trials, and the block coder then discards the transform because
block sorting reads the plain bytes (`blocks.c`: "max: text to BWT"). Its only effect on the frame is the plan byte
(8 or 0) of a block whose payload is the same. Time after the `table_log_ms` line until the plan is returned:
dickens 173 ms of 1,726 (10.0%), reymont 103 of 707 (14.5%), xml 75 of 361 (20.8%), webster 138 of 5,602 (2.5%).

(The CM coder's own preset race runs inside the one `codec=7` call and is therefore inside "kept" here; it is costed

- **osdb: 97.6% of the `-x` encode is discarded.** Context mixing takes 47.5 s and writes 2,217,609 B; block sorting
  then takes 1.07 s and writes 2,206,522 B, which is kept. An encoder that had seen this kind of data before would
  code the cheap candidate first and know that the expensive one does not beat it.
- **Text: the planner codes two 1 MiB samples with context mixing for a plan the block coder ignores.** `make_plan`
  returns the word plan (t=8) for xml and dickens; `ultra_keep_transform` then codes the word-coded sample and the
  raw sample with CM to decide whether to keep it; the block coder discards the word transform at `-x` whatever the
  answer ("ultra: CM models words itself"). `plan_ms`: xml 7,020 of 51,429 ms (13.7%), dickens 7,883 of 81,095 ms
  (9.7%); reymont, whose word trial lost at the zstd stage, 149 ms. The cost is fixed (two 1 MiB CM passes), so it
  grows as the file shrinks (2.5: 1 MiB text files).
- mr, x-ray, sao, ooffice: 8 to 17% in LZMA2 and block-sorting alternatives that lose to CM by 2 to 19%.

Images (`run_tax.sh '-x' ximg`, the 17 scikit-image PPM/PGM files of `/home/user/kolmo-review40-data/R05-img/pnm`,
