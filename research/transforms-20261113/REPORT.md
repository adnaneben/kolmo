# Small transforms compound only through enablers

Kolmo still has about fifteen cheap, reversible additions with published gains, but the evidence says a small isolated gain predicts its stacked gain poorly in both directions, so each one has to be re-measured with its successor stages and its leaf coder attached. Stacking amplifies in two measured situations: when one transform is an enabler whose benefit only a specific successor can bank (shuffle, then per-plane delta, then per-plane entropy coding lifts ERA5 pressure from 4.0x to 5.7x), and when two transforms remove different redundancy (dictionary gives 7x on strings and FSST adds a further 51% on top). Stacking cancels when both stages target the same redundancy (Parquet's v2 encodings save 30% ahead of Snappy but 11% ahead of zstd) and goes negative when a transform destroys structure its successor needs (byte-stream split before zstd fell to 0.89x of plain on one float32 file). The best-evidenced candidates not yet in kolmo are static order-1 rANS (6 to 20% smaller than order-0 on correlated small-alphabet streams), a dictionary/RLE cascade after the decimal node (ALP's own table moves from 21.7 to 18.8 bits per value), byte-delta inside shuffled planes (median 5.86x against 5.1x for shuffle alone), per-architecture branch filters for ARM64 and RISC-V (5% and 7 to 8% with LZMA2), cross-column equality/map/reference nodes (about 8% at table level, 30 to 58% on single date columns) and a pco-style binning leaf. Stack choice is cheap: every production system trials a small candidate pool on roughly 1% of the data and loses 1 to 3.3% to the optimum, and nothing published supports reinforcement learning for this job. The evidence base has real holes, stated at the end: no published ablation of cascade depth, none of entropy order after delta plus shuffle, and several primary sources (kkrunchy, 7-Zip BCJ2 documentation, the zstd long-range release notes, precomp, the FSST paper) were blocked.

## Stacked gains fall into four measured classes

Lossless transforms are bijections, so they add nothing by themselves; all gain comes from making the remaining redundancy visible to the coder that follows. The cleanest proof is the Burrows-Wheeler transform: it is provably worth exactly zero to a static or adaptive order-0 coder (SOURCES stays at 69.48% with or without it), while a locally adaptive coder goes **from 64.20% to 26.44%** on the same file, and only from 24.64% to 23.02% on DNA ([Weighted Burrows-Wheeler Compression](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.10327)). A transform's value is therefore a property of the transform, the coder and the data together. Pcodec makes the same point from the other side: splitting a value into two latent streams can raise theoretical entropy and still shrink the output because each part is modelled better ([Pcodec paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06112v2)). The practical rule for kolmo's planner follows directly: the only valid cost of an intermediate stream is its size after the actual leaf coder, never its empirical entropy.

The measured cases sort into four classes. This taxonomy is the researchers' synthesis from individual results; no paper found formally characterises when composed lossless transforms are super-additive.

**Enabler pairs are super-additive.** The first stage does little or nothing until a specific successor exists. On ERA5 float data, the median ratio across codecs rises from 3.86x unfiltered to 5.1x with shuffle and **5.86x with shuffle plus byte-delta**; on the pressure set under zstd level 9, bytedelta reaches 5.0x against 4.0x for bitshuffle, and coding the byte planes as separate streams lifts it to 5.7x against 4.1x ([Blosc bytedelta post](https://blosc.org/posts/bytedelta-enhance-compression-toolset/)). Splitting the streams adds 14% to bytedelta and about 2.5% to bitshuffle, so the same last step is worth five times more behind the right predecessor. On OSM Belgium longitudes, LZ4 alone achieves 1.00x and byte-stream split plus LZ4 achieves 1.93x ([PARQUET-2414](https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/PARQUET-2414)). TileDB documents the canonical order, positive-delta before bit-width reduction, because the delta creates the narrow values that only the reducer can bank ([TileDB filters tutorial](https://tiledb-inc-tiledb.readthedocs-hosted.com/en/1.6.3/tutorials/filters.html)).

**Independent pairs are roughly multiplicative.** In BtrBlocks' ablation, dictionary coding gives about 7x on strings and FSST on the dictionary a further 51%, because one removes whole-value repetition and the other substring repetition ([BtrBlocks paper](https://www.cs.cit.tum.de:443/fileadmin/w00cfj/dis/papers/btrblocks.pdf)). A light cascade followed by a heavy back end behaves the same way: Vortex on TPC-H SF10 goes from 2,776 MB with light encodings to 2,016 MB once PCodec and zstd are added, a further 27% ([Vortex blog](https://vortex.dev/blog/btrblocks-compressor)). Word-dictionary transforms and strong text models also stack: a tuned static dictionary still improves ppmonstr on enwik8 from 19,098,634 to 18,154,921 bytes ([Large Text Compression Benchmark](https://mattmahoney.net/dc/text.html)).

**Substitute pairs partly cancel.** Enabling Parquet v2 encodings on TPC-H lineitem makes Snappy files about 30% smaller and zstd files about 11% smaller, because zstd already captures part of what the encodings remove ([DuckDB blog](https://duckdb.org/2025/01/22/parquet-encodings)). FastLanes' leave-one-out ablation shows the same heavy tail: Dictionary is worth **42.36%**, DELTA 5.92%, and RLE only 0.69%, since dictionary plus bit-packing covers most of RLE's cases when RLE is removed ([FastLanes file format paper](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35881/35881.pdf)). Word-dictionary gains shrink as the back end strengthens: on large text the PPM-oriented WRT gives gzip 15.57%, bzip2 12.07%, PPMd 9.71% and PAQ6 7.93% ([Revisiting dictionary-based compression](https://www.academia.edu/5675934/Revisiting_dictionary_based_compression)). XML-aware front ends improved on their back end by at most about 5% in one comparative study ([Sakr, XML compression tools](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0806.0075)).

**Destructive pairs go below baseline.** Byte-stream split lowered zstd's ratio to 0.89x of plain on a float32 file with whole-value repeats ([PARQUET-2414](https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/PARQUET-2414)). Blosc's older XOR-style "delta" combined with shuffle stayed under 2.0x everywhere, worse than unfiltered zstd or LZ4 ([Float Compression 8: Blosc](https://aras-p.info/blog/2023/03/02/Float-Compression-8-Blosc/)). Bytedelta worsens the ERA5 precipitation set ([Blosc bytedelta post](https://blosc.org/posts/bytedelta-enhance-compression-toolset/)). A second delta pass on lena.bmp turned 511,316 bytes back into 645,634 ([Data Compression Explained](https://mattmahoney.net/dc/dce.html)). XMill-style container separation helps gzip and bounded-context PPM by up to 15% but worsens bzip2 and PPM* ([XMLPPM paper](https://xmlppm.sourceforge.net/paper/node8.html)), and a BPE tokeniser alone costs PPMd 1.64 points until the ids are frequency-ordered ([Frequency-Ordered Tokenization](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22958v1)).

Order among small polishing stages matters far less than the choice of the big transform. Inside BWT pipelines, run-length coding before weighted frequency count gives 2.241 bps and after it 2.271 bps, a 1.3% difference ([survey reproducing Abel's tables](https://www.cs.brandeis.edu/~dilant/cs175/%5BDan-Preston%5D.pdf)); Fenwick's whole sequence of post-BWT coder refinements moves Calgary from 2.52 to 2.34 bits per byte ([Fenwick, Block sorting text compression](https://www.cs.auckland.ac.nz/~peter-f/FTPfiles/1997%20CompJourn%20BWT.pdf)). The amplification the owner is looking for is real, then, but conditional. A small gain amplifies when it narrows a range or alphabet ahead of a width-sensitive stage, separates streams with different statistics ahead of a per-stream entropy coder, or aligns equal bytes ahead of LZ. It evaporates when the successor is a strong model that already sees the same dependency. A cheap diagnostic follows: measure each candidate once ahead of an order-0 coder and once ahead of the strongest leaf. A gain that collapses under the strong leaf is a substitute; one that holds or grows is a stacking candidate.

## Numeric and entropy-stage additions with measured gains

**Order-1 static rANS is the best-documented step beyond kolmo's order-0 coder.** On two real field streams, order-1 is 19.6% smaller than order-0 for a 40-symbol alphabet and 5.9% smaller for an 8-symbol one, decoding at 366 to 478 MB/s scalar and about **1,000 MB/s with 32-way AVX2 interleaving** on a 2013 Haswell; static arithmetic coding with the same model is within 0.6% in size and 4 to 10 times slower ([rans_static benchmarks](https://github.com/da0ka/rans_static)). The gain lives in the context, not the coder: on book1, Huffman costs only 0.64 to 0.73% over rANS at order-0 ([sserangecoding README](https://github.com/richgel999/sserangecoding)). CRAM 3.1 shows how to keep order-1 cheap: 10-bit frequencies for cache fit, the order-1 table itself compressed with order-0, a raw fallback for short blocks, and per-block flags so the encoder picks the combination ([CRAM codec specification 3.1](https://cmdcolin.github.io/hts-specs-md/CRAMcodecs)). Order selection needs no trial coding, since both sizes fall out of the order-0 and order-1 histograms in one pass. Against the fast-mode reference (zstd -2 decodes silesia.tar at 1,246 MB/s in [lzbench](https://github.com/inikep/lzbench)), only the SIMD variant fits, and only per stream. No source measures order-1 specifically on shuffled, delta-coded planes, so the 6 to 20% is an upper range for kolmo.

The same CRAM stack supplies three near-free wrappers kolmo's list lacks: PACK (alphabets of 16 or fewer symbols packed 2, 4 or 8 per byte before the entropy coder, which turns an order-1 byte context into a longer symbol context), run-length coding restricted to chosen symbols with runs in a side stream, and stream elision when a stream is constant or duplicates an earlier one ([CRAM codec specification 3.1](https://cmdcolin.github.io/hts-specs-md/CRAMcodecs)). This differs from the bit-packing kolmo dropped, because the packed bytes are still entropy coded. The spec gives no isolated gains; the whole CRAM 3.1 codec set is 7 to 16% smaller than CRAM 3.0 ([CRAM 3.1 preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.09.15.460485v1.full.pdf)).

**The decimal node should feed dictionary and RLE, not only frame-of-reference.** In ALP's own table plain zstd (20.6 bits per value) beats plain ALP (21.7) on the all-dataset average; ALP passes zstd only when cascaded with dictionary or RLE, at **18.8 bits** ([ALP paper](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/33334/33334.pdf)). That 13% is the cheapest numeric experiment available, because it is a wiring check on nodes kolmo already owns. The same table confirms the dropped schemes should stay dropped: zstd alone beats Gorilla (42.2), Chimp (37.7), Chimp128 (28.7) and Patas (35.5), and ALP-RD loses to zstd on both high-precision coordinate columns.

**Byte-delta inside planes and data-driven plane grouping extend the existing shuffle.** Bytedelta's median 5.86x against 5.1x is a 15% gain over shuffle, concentrated in zstd-class back ends; LZ4 and BloscLZ gain little, zstd at levels 6 and 9 can need up to twice the compression time, and decompression slows by up to about 20% ([Blosc bytedelta post](https://blosc.org/posts/bytedelta-enhance-compression-toolset/)). An independent 94.5 MB float32 test took zstd from 33.8 MB to about 23 MB with split bytes plus delta ([Float Compression 3: Filters](https://aras-p.info/blog/2023/02/01/Float-Compression-3-Filters/)). Typed Data Transformation clusters byte positions by entropy and emits each cluster as one stream, for a geomean 1.04x to 1.20x across back ends, but it loses on some columns and loses badly under bzip2 (2.170 against 3.130), so it helps LZ leaves more than BWT leaves ([TDT paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18062v2)). Bitshuffle remains worth having as a selectable alternative: FCBench ranks bitshuffle plus zstd first of 13 methods on 33 float datasets at 1.466 overall, though it tested neither byte shuffle nor bytedelta ([FCBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10301v2)). The same benchmark sets expectations for genuinely full-precision doubles: the median ratio is 1.202, and nothing reaches much past 1.6x.

**Pcodec contributes a leaf and three detectors.** Its pipeline is mode split, then delta of order 1 to 7 chosen by trial, then binning: an entropy-coded bin index plus raw offset bits within the bin, which avoids the loss byte planes take when the informative bits straddle a byte boundary. It reports 29 to 94% better ratio than the best byte-oriented alternative on six datasets at over 1 GiB/s decode, with no ablation and no comparison against ALP or OpenZL, so those figures are an upper bound ([Pcodec paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06112v2)). Its modes generalise nodes kolmo has: IntMult splits x into quotient and remainder streams and so tolerates noisy remainders that defeat plain gcd division; FloatMult handles floats that are multiples of an arbitrary base; FloatQuant shifts off mostly-zero low mantissa bits and keeps a secondary stream instead of an exception list ([pco float_quant.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/pco/latest/source/src/mode/float_quant.rs)). Pcodec's lookback delta and OpenZL's Field LZ are the same idea, LZ at value granularity ([OpenZL paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03203)). It is the natural complement to byte planes, since an LZ stage cannot find a repeated 8-byte value once its bytes sit in different planes, but neither source publishes an isolated gain.

**Cross-column nodes beyond formula search show the largest column-level numbers.** Corra saves 58.3% on l_receiptdate coded against l_shipdate, 30.6% on taxi drop-off against pick-up and 53.7% on zip against city, relative to the best single-column encoding ([Corra](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17229v2)). C3 reports 1.2x at table level on a Public BI subset ([C3 thesis](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~boncz/msc/2023-ThomasGlas.pdf)), and FastLanes attributes about 8% ratio and 20% decode speed to equality, one-to-one map and cast together ([FastLanes file format paper](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35881/35881.pdf)). Kolmo's CSV formula search already covers the arithmetic cases; the open increments are equality-with-exceptions, one-to-one dictionary maps, per-key frame of reference and hierarchical pairs, and their extension to non-CSV columnar inputs. All of these figures are measured against light encodings, not after zstd or BWT.

On the archive side, **LZMA2 literal and position bits are a decode-free knob**: lc0:lp2:pb3 was 1.84% smaller than the default on a binary game set ([Oodle LZNA post](https://cbloomrants.blogspot.com/2015/05/05-09-15-oodle-lzna.html)), and the xz maintainer measured 0 to 2% from matching lp and lc to instruction alignment ([LKML xz patch](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/20240320183846.19475-12-lasse.collin@tukaani.org/)). Three candidates do not earn a place. Alternative BWT post-stages are within 0.86% of libbsc on enwik8 at 78 times the compression time, and PPMd is both larger and slower than bsc on enwik9 ([Large Text Compression Benchmark](https://mattmahoney.net/dc/text.html)). Frequency encoding measured 0.09% in FastLanes and was removed ([FastLanes file format paper](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35881/35881.pdf)). LeCo's linear-model residuals gain 40.9% over frame-of-reference on smooth data but claim only parity with delta on real data ([LeCo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.15374v3.pdf)), and kolmo already has delta and second difference.

## Binary, text and structured-data additions

**Branch filters for other instruction sets are the firmest binary result.** With LZMA2 on Linux kernel images, the ARM64 filter cuts size about 5%, the RISC-V filter 7 to 8%, and switching Thumb-2 code from the ARM filter to the Thumb filter about 5%; the wrong filter "didn't do anything useful" ([LKML xz patch](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/20240320183846.19475-12-lasse.collin@tukaani.org/)). Those are near-pure code; on a mixed Fedora image the x86 filter saved about 1.8% ([Wikipedia: BCJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCJ_(algorithm))). The next step for x86 is BCJ2-style splitting, which sends call and jump displacements to their own streams with a range-coded selector ([7z format specification, filters](https://zesven.pages.dev/7z/11-filters)); no sourced number for its gain over plain E8/E9 was obtained. FreeArc's automatic table-detecting delta filter improved binary files by about 2% when introduced ([FreeArc 0.40 notes](https://freearc.sourceforge.net/Presenting%20FreeArc%200.40.htm)), which is the increment available from finding tables at arbitrary offsets inside larger files.

Two heavier items are routes, not filters, and belong only in archive modes. Lepton recompresses JPEGs losslessly to 77% of original size where deflate, LZMA, zstd and Brotli manage 1% or less, with about 6% of real-world inputs needing a verbatim fallback ([Lepton paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1704.06192)). Preflate reconstructs any zlib-made deflate stream bit-exactly from the plaintext plus a few bytes to a few KiB of side data, but its single-thread speed is described by its author as poor ([preflate README](https://github.com/deus-libri/preflate)). Long-range deduplication is plausibly valuable and back-end-agnostic, but this research produced no isolated measurement for it.

**For text, the unexploited gains are in variants and sub-techniques of the dictionary transform kolmo already has.** The variants conflict: the LZ77-tuned WRT (dense codewords, spaces removed) gives gzip 27.32% on large files but PAQ6 only 4.77%, and 0.27% on Calgary, while the PPM-tuned variant gives PAQ6 7.93% ([Revisiting dictionary-based compression](https://www.academia.edu/5675934/Revisiting_dictionary_based_compression)). One variant per leaf family, chosen by the planner, is therefore required. Dictionary ordering is the lever specific to context mixing: ppmonstr on enwik8 improves 3.2% with XWRT's semi-dynamic per-file dictionary and 4.9% with the tuned, semantically grouped paq8hp5 static dictionary ([Large Text Compression Benchmark](https://mattmahoney.net/dc/text.html)). The semi-dynamic form matters for non-English text, where a static English dictionary does nothing. For non-Latin scripts, feeding a PPM coder code points instead of UTF-8 bytes is worth about 6.1% ([Unicode text compression](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.04047)).

**For logs and JSON lines, the gain sits in the variables, not the template.** At Uber, CLP's row-wise template form plus zstd is only 5 to 8% better than zstd on raw logs, while the columnar pipeline is 2.16x better ([Uber on CLP](https://www.uber.com/blog/reducing-logging-cost-by-two-orders-of-magnitude-using-clp)); LogShrink's column-oriented layout is 36 to 103% smaller than row-oriented ([LogShrink](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.09479)). Typed numeric token handling stacks on top of mature parsers: Denum's module raises LogReducer's average ratio from 60.928 to 74.240 (+17%) and LogShrink's from 74.091 to 78.490 (+6%), while regressing 2 and 3 of 16 datasets by 4 to 19%, so it must be selected per group by measured cost ([Denum](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.05760v1)). DeLog's shape-and-context token signatures beat Denum on Loghub (88.056 against 80.568) and its authors state that higher parsing accuracy does not guarantee better compression ([DeLog](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15084)). The cheapest fast-tier candidate is LogLite's parser-free trick, which zeroes bytes equal to a recent line of the same length and run-length codes them: it beats LZMA alone by 37.7% on JSON logs and 17.1% on text logs ([LogLite](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10337v1)). Every one of these results uses an LZMA or zstd back end; none tests BWT or context mixing, and the XML evidence above warns that container splitting can hurt exactly those leaves. For genomics, NAF's stream split with 4-bit nucleotide packing and plain zstd lands close to dedicated context-model tools on the human genome while decompressing 35 to 78 times faster ([NAF paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6761962)).

## A sampled trial over a small pool lands within 3% of optimal

Every production system found chooses its stack by trial on small samples or by offline search plus a small online pool. BtrBlocks trial-compresses 10 runs of 64 values per 64,000-value block (1%), is optimal or within 2% on 77% of choices, loses 3.3% on average and spends 1.2% of compression time selecting ([BtrBlocks paper](https://www.cs.cit.tum.de:443/fileadmin/w00cfj/dis/papers/btrblocks.pdf)). FastLanes runs six ordered rules, then tries a pool of about 31 expressions (mined once by exhaustive search on Public BI) on the first, middle and last 1,024-value vectors, and reaches **over 99% of the best ratio** ([FastLanes file format paper](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35881/35881.pdf)). OpenZL's trainer, a greedy cluster merge followed by an NSGA-II genetic search, captures a 29% gain from the first 1% of the data, with the remaining 99% adding about 3%; it runs offline at 1.12 to 11.6 MiB per minute ([OpenZL second paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09928)).

Four design points carry over. First, estimate through the whole downstream cascade, as Vortex does, because a per-node estimate cannot see interaction effects; use closed-form costs only for schemes whose size is a function of simple statistics, such as frame of reference and dictionary ([Vortex blog](https://vortex.dev/blog/btrblocks-compressor)). Second, samples must preserve adjacency: CodecDB's accuracy fell to 65% with random instead of contiguous sampling ([CodecDB](https://thedatumorg.github.io/DATUM/papers/JiangSIGMOD21.pdf)). Third, guards do the pruning: Pseudodecimal is disabled above 50% exceptions or below 10% unique values, run-end encoding needs an average run of at least 4, dictionary needs at most 50% distinct values, pcodec stops raising delta order when order o costs more than o-1, and mksquashfs tries each BCJ filter per block and keeps the best ([BtrBlocks paper](https://www.cs.cit.tum.de:443/fileadmin/w00cfj/dis/papers/btrblocks.pdf); [Vortex blog](https://vortex.dev/blog/btrblocks-compressor); [Pcodec paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06112v2); [Slax blog](https://www.slax.org/blog/18663-XZ-compression-filters)). Fourth, kolmo's rule of taking the smallest option whose cost stays inside the mode budget has a production precedent in Oodle, which prices bytes saved against decode time and switches entropy coding on or off per stream ([cbloom rants 2018](https://cbloomrants.blogspot.com/2018)).

Reinforcement learning is not needed for ratio. The one documented use, Alibaba's AMMMO time-series compressor, trains a policy over 331,776 parameter combinations and lands "very close to or even better" than a 5,000-sample random search ([AMMMO paper](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~lifeifei/papers/MLCompression.pdf)). With cheap selection already within 1 to 3.3% of optimal, a learned policy can only save selection time. The absence of RL in any shipped general-purpose or columnar system rests on about 25 searches, not on proof. The pattern that fits kolmo's zstd -3 budget is the FastLanes one: discover enabling chains offline, freeze a short typed pool, trial only the pool. What nobody has published is the regret of greedy top-down cascade choice against beam or exhaustive search; BtrBlocks' 3.3% conflates sampling error with greedy error, so a kolmo measurement at depth 3 would be new data. OpenZL's averaged speed of 142 MiB/s compress and 323 MiB/s decompress is the archive-mode cost line these trials have to fit under ([OpenZL second paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09928)).

Expected gains are the published figures, which are upper ranges for kolmo because most were measured against weaker pipelines than kolmo's existing one. ## Where the sources were missing or blocked

Several questions in the brief are unanswered by published evidence. No paper or blog reports a depth-1 against depth-2 against depth-3 cascade ablation. Nothing measures order-1 or adaptive entropy coding specifically on byte-shuffled, delta-coded numeric planes. Chimp, Elf or Patas output followed by zstd or BWT is unmeasured, so Elf-style erasure as a pre-transform is unverified. No single benchmark ranks byte shuffle, bytedelta, ALP, pco and Elf on full-precision data. No log, JSON or CSV transform is tested ahead of BWT or context-mixing leaves. Nothing was found on CSV-specific transforms beyond what kolmo has, on packing UUIDs, hex strings or IP addresses to binary, on base64 decoding gains, or on source-code transforms beyond one 98 KB Python file. OpenZL publishes neither a per-codec ablation nor the codec chains its trainer found.

Specific sources could not be read. Blocked by the fetch permission layer: Fabian Giesen's kkrunchy x86 article (the best single source on disassembly-based stream splitting), the 7-Zip method documentation (BCJ2), the zstd v1.3.2 release notes (long-distance matching), the precomp, brunsli, libbsc, Turbo-Range-Coder and kanzi-cpp repositories, the htscodecs benchmark page, the xz NEWS file, the pco format specification and Wikipedia's rzip page. ## Conclusion

The owner's hypothesis that small gains amplify when stacked holds for enabler chains and for transforms that remove different redundancy, and fails for substitutes and for transforms that strip what the successor needs. That changes what an experiment log should record. A candidate's isolated percentage is nearly uninformative; the useful record is a pair of measurements, ahead of an order-0 coder and ahead of the strongest leaf, plus the class it lands in with each existing node. Candidates whose gain collapses under the strong leaf belong in the fast tier only, where the leaf is weak and the transform is doing the modelling. Candidates whose gain holds are the ones worth stacking further.

The literature is thinnest where kolmo is strongest. Columnar systems measure light cascades without a heavy leaf, log compressors measure only LZMA, and general-purpose compressors never ablate numeric transforms. Kolmo, with typed transforms in front of zstd, BWT, LZMA2 and context-mixing leaves, sits in the unmeasured middle, so its own corpus results on the top five experiments (cascade after the decimal node, order-1 rANS, per-plane byte-delta, branch filters, cross-column nodes) would be new evidence and not a replication. The cheapest structural move is the FastLanes one: mine the enabling chains offline once, freeze them as a typed pool of a few dozen expressions, and let the in-budget planner trial only those on a 1% adjacency-preserving sample.
