# Impact matrix: every research codec, transform and model × data kind × level × threads

Branch `claude/impact-matrix-20261124`, from origin/main `896dc6d6`. Apple M5, 10 cores, macOS. 25 November 2026.
Method, harness and corpus: `METHOD.md`. What exists and where it runs today: `INVENTORY.md`. Every row:
`raw/rows.jsonl` (one JSON object per feature × file × level × thread count). Per-kind tables for every feature:
`tables.md` (generated by `overview.py` and `summarize.py` from the rows).

**Scope of the numbers.** 53 real files cut to ≤ 4 MiB, 15 kinds, dev and holdout kept apart, plus 62 small cuts
(16 KiB, 128 KiB, 1 MiB) of the text-like files for the priors. Levels 1-9 and ultra (`-U`), `-T 1` and `-T 10`.
Speed: wall time and peak RSS of whole processes (`/usr/bin/time -l`), one cell at a time under the machine-wide lock; every feature row was taken at load average below 20 (cells first timed above it were re-timed, their first rows kept in `raw/rows-highload.jsonl` with identical frames), and speed ratios are against `base2`, the level's own frames re-timed at load 1.4-3.0. Frames do not depend on the thread count: all 4559 T1/T10 frame pairs are identical, so sizes are
given once. Every frame decoded back to its input (SHA-256): 0 failures in 11107 rows.

"kept" = the frame with the feature as a per-block candidate (used only where smaller) against the level's own frame;
"forced" = the feature on every block it applies to. Negative is smaller.

## Contradictions with earlier keep/reject decisions

1. **rc3w3 priors and lexer: "DEFER, superseded by W4 cm"** (`$V/evidence/FEATURE_DECISIONS.md`). The deferral was
   about the codec-id collision, and W4's model 4 was taken to replace it. On main's model 4 the lexer contexts still
   gain 1-3.7% on every text kind at ultra, and the priors 1-6% under 128 KiB: they are not superseded.
2. **Levels 1-2 use minimal analysis** (`src/api/api.c` level table: "minimal analysis, 0.0.1 -m speed exactly"; L2
   "screening 3.211, 433 / 835 MiB/s"). On tables, logs, numeric arrays, 16-bit images, weights and x86 code, full
   analysis makes the frame 8-75% smaller for about 1.7× the compress time and 1.2-1.3× the decode time. The screening
   suite that chose minimal analysis had few such files.
3. **Level 8 = block sorting for every block** (0.0.1 "ratio -l 1"). KLZMA is smaller *and* decodes faster on an
   ARM64 binary, an XLS, tar mixes, and on the table/log/numeric transform streams (holdout and dev alike).
4. **GR6+: "won only on tables with derived columns; prose, logs and JSON never chose it"** (`gr6.c` gate comment,
   `benchmark/rc3/gr6`). True at ultra, where CM beats it. Below ultra GR6+ wins on space-separated logs (BGL, NASA
   access log) by 4-82%; the gate also admits them as tables (space separator).
5. **"The table transform pre-empts the log trial"** (SYNTHESIS #17, BGL +152% at -3): at L3 the transforms still
   help BGL, but at L9 the frame is 6.7% smaller with no transform at all.

## Routing proposal

Each line: the exact kinds and levels, the evidence (rows in `tables.md`), the cost, and what it would change. None of
this is implemented here: every route changes frames (the determinism list) and the first two change the format.

1. **Lexer token-class contexts → ultra, every CM stream that `looks_text` admits** (all text kinds, every size).
   Evidence: 26 text-like 4 MiB files and 62 small cuts at `-U`, dev and holdout alike; gains 0.1-3.7% (code and logs
   most), the worst cell +0.19% (one 16 KiB JSON). Cost: 1.06-1.22× the compress and decode time of `-U` (medians, T1 and T10; worst file 1.59×), peak memory 1.01-1.05×. Needs: model 4 accepting mask bits 30-31 in the
   decoder (a format decision; no new codec id), the lane's hashing code (ported in `src/codecs/cm.c` under
   `KOLMO_IMPACT`), `CM4_MASK_TEXT | 0xC0000000`.
2. **Per-kind priors → ultra, text streams under 256 KiB whose kind `text_kind` recognises** (markup, JSON, CSV, log,
   C, Python, JavaScript, prose), together with route 1. Evidence: 62 small cuts; 16 KiB −2.3 to −7.2%, 128 KiB −1.3
   to −3.4% with the lexer; from 1 MiB the prior alone is flat to +0.47%, so the 4 MiB limit of the lane is too high.
   Cost: 1.2× peak memory, 3 MB of tables in the library (388 KB packed by the lane with liblzma, which main no longer
   has: they would need repacking with KLZMA). Needs: prior ids 3-10 in model 4's tune byte (format decision).
3. **Full analysis at level 2 for every input, and at level 1 for delimited text, logs, numeric arrays and WAV**
   (not 8-bit images: +0.7 to +27%; not video: +3.7 to +5.1%). Evidence: `tf-full` rows, dev and holdout; CSV/TSV
   −15 to −75%, logs −8 to −29%, numeric −4 to −57%, img16 −26 to −38% (L2), weights −8 to −15% (L2), x86 binary −11% (L2), WAV −14 to −23% (L1). Cost: about 1.7× the compress time (1.1-3.1×) and 1.2-1.3× the decode time (the inverse transforms cost more than the smaller payload saves; 0.8-2.0×), peak compress memory 1.5-3.1×; still 36-800 MB/s decode. Changes the level table rows 1-2 (`src/api/api.c`).
4. **Level 8: KLZMA as a candidate beside BWT** on (a) binary blocks (executables, tar mixes), gated by the
   slice predictor level 9 already uses, and (b) the table and log transform streams (tflags 4 and 5) and numeric
   streams. Evidence (holdout): ARM64 dylib −19.4%, BGL log −33.0%, bb rows TSV −12.1%, backblaze CSV −6.9%, sao −5.2%,
   mozilla tar −0.7%; dev: ghcn rows −32.0%, kennedy.xls −23.8%, canterbury tar −4.7%; each decodes faster than the
   BWT frame (0.29-0.78× the time, decode memory 0.3-0.6×). Cost: 4.5-7× the compress time of those blocks (1.7-14×), peak compress memory 1.9×. The held-out PDFs and JAR gain nothing.
5. **GR6+: no change (ultra only).** Below ultra it is smaller everywhere its gate admits a block (L1-3 −16 to −84%,
   L8 −3.6 to −41%, L9 −6 to −13%) but decodes at 3-11 MB/s with 6-25× the memory, which no level below ultra allows.
   At ultra, CM beats it on every gated file except owid-co2 (where ultra already picks it). A "tables tier" between
   9 and ultra (GR6+ for gated blocks, decode about 5 MB/s) is an owner decision, not a routing fix.
6. **Plain CM, BWT and KLZMA below their levels: no change.** They are smaller (CM up to −95%) but decode at
   0.00-0.09× (CM) or 0.1-0.8× (BWT, KLZMA) the level's speed; that is the next level's trade-off. KLZH2 9 at
   levels 1-5 is smaller at the same decode speed but compresses 7-78× more slowly (the effort ladder).
7. **Level 9, space-separated logs: try no transform as well.** BGL at L9: −6.7% with no transform (the table
   transform takes the log, SYNTHESIS #17). One file: confirm on more logs before changing the trial order.
8. **Stacking** (the level's transform output coded by another coder, against that coder on the plain bytes, L3
