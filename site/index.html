Kolmo benchmarks

Kolmo 0.0.2 against the standard codecs, against OpenZL with and without training, and against ML-weight compressors. Every number on this page comes from bench/repro and can be reproduced with one command. Losses are listed next to wins, and every verdict is computed from the data, not written by hand.

Built 2026-11-24. Machine: Apple M5 (arm64, 10 cores), Darwin 27.2.0. One thread per tool unless a row says otherwise. x86-64 results come from the bench-x86 workflow.

Structured data: Kolmo (zero configuration) vs OpenZL, untrained and trained

Source: Apple M5 (arm64, 10 cores), macOS, 2026-11-23/24, kolmo v0.0.2, OpenZL 50f8789a trained and untrained.

OpenZL is Meta’s format-aware framework; its best results come from training (zli train) on samples of the data. Here OpenZL gets its matching profile at levels 6 and 9 (untrained), and a compressor trained on a separate training split of the same kind of data (trained); the training files are never measured. Kolmo gets no profile, no schema and no training. The holdout sets are public data new to Kolmo’s development. “Kolmo best” is often -9 or -U, which decode at 1–400 MiB/s; compare the -3 and -5 columns for everyday use.

ML weights

Model files compress poorly with every general tool: the gain comes from the exponent bits. ZipNN and DFloat11 are built for exactly this. DFloat11’s decoder needs an NVIDIA GPU; its column is the size its published method gives, computed on the CPU (bench/repro/dfloat11_tool.py), not a run of DFloat11 itself. ZipNN times are in-process (Python start-up excluded).

Method

  • One thread for every tool (lzbench’s NO_THREADING, -T1/-j1 on the command line), except the rows marked -T0 or -jN in the multi-thread table.
  • Same compiler flags. Kolmo is built with make ARCH= (generic -O3, no -mcpu), the flags of its release binaries; lzbench builds every codec, Kolmo included, with its own flags. OpenZL is a CMake Release build.
  • Pinned versions: lzbench 9be0e25505, OpenZL 50f8789a0c (zli 0.3.0), Kolmo 550c02fd89d0, CLIs bzip3 1.5.5, xz 5.8.4, zstd 1.5.7.
  • Verified: every round trip is compared byte for byte; a failure is shown as an error, never dropped.
  • Best of 3 for command-line runs (one run above 60 MB or for the slowest settings); lzbench: best of 3 × 3 s for fast codecs, one run for slow ones.
  • Serialised: each timed workload holds a machine-wide lock, so no two benchmarks run at once.
  • Data: every input is fetched from its source and checked by sha256 (bench/repro/datasets.json, bench/lzbench/corpora.py); nothing is redistributed.

Caveats

  • One machine per page build. These numbers are from the machine above. x86-64 numbers come from a separate workflow run.
  • OpenZL training used zli train’s default (greedy) trainer with a 20-minute cap per profile, on four training files per set. More training data or time may help OpenZL further.
  • Holdout data is new to Kolmo, but Silesia, enwik8 and Canterbury were seen in earlier tuning, and the development panel overlaps the development data.
  • DFloat11 was not run (it needs CUDA); its column is computed from its method. cmix, paq8px and nncp were not run.
  • Levels are being tuned before launch; numbers will be regenerated with the same command.
